## Submission requirements

Under the [2026 Project Submission Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/):

- Window: November 14, 09:00 through November 15, 23:59 in the registered event's timezone. Universal Event uses the Team Owner's residence timezone. Romanian events use Europe/Bucharest.
- Complete the English team Project page: summary, description, final-project link, demo, NASA resources, references, and AI/SI usage.
- Public demo: video at most 30 seconds OR deck at most 7 slides, including title/intro. Linked materials must be English; non-English demo speech needs English subtitles.
- Demo and final-project links must open without login or permission requests.
- At least one NASA resource/data source is required for Global Award eligibility.
- Disclose AI-generated code/data in descriptive text and metadata; label generated images/videos visibly and audio in text/metadata. Generated content must not incorporate or alter NASA branding. AI may assist execution; the solution idea must come from the team.
- Accept the required declarations and click **Submit for Judging** before the deadline. Saving progress alone does not submit.

## Rights, credits, and privacy

The [2026 Participant Terms](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/) require original contributions to be unrestricted or OSI open source; this repo uses MIT. Cite every external source, including freely available code/images, and document third-party licenses. Do not submit material you lack rights to publish, sensitive information, or personal identifying information. Do not include recognizable names, voices, images, or likenesses of under-18s in submissions or demos. Obtain adult consent where applicable.

**Minecraft eligibility question:** the terms also impose open availability/open-source conditions on non-NASA solution materials. Minecraft is proprietary and requires separate ownership. Our mod's MIT license does not settle that requirement. Confirm with the Local Lead whether a Minecraft-dependent mod is acceptable for the award track. Public source and accessible video/slides let judges inspect the work without installing the game, but do not establish eligibility. Never bundle Minecraft binaries or assets in the submission.

## What judges score

The [2026 Judging & Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/) scores each category from 1–5: impact, creativity, validity, relevance, and presentation. Show a learning outcome, scientifically defensible trade-offs, meaningful NASA resource use, and an understandable demo. Local selection and Global Judging are separate stages; an eligible upload alone does not advance a project.

## Challenge target

Official brief: [Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/).

Build an interactive outpost game for ages 7–12 that teaches consequences of resource decisions. Objectives cover life support, radiation protection, electricity, growing food, and communication delay. Prioritize understandable cause and effect. The format is open; a Minecraft mod is our team's implementation, subject to the licensing question above.

The brief encourages NASA open data. Suggestions include [DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/), [OSDR](https://osdr.nasa.gov/), [RadLab](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/), and [Mars Trek](https://trek.nasa.gov/mars/). Static, attributed real data is a possible starting point; do not call it live. Distinguish game balance assumptions from scientific measurements.
